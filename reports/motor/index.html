Prius motor study

Can an open-source toolchain (Pyleecan and FEMM for the electromagnetics, CalculiX for the rotor and the slot heat check, a lumped thermal network) predict a production traction motor well enough for early design screening? The test case is the 2004 Prius motor, which Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL) took apart and measured.

The question and the limits

Two kinds of answer come out of this study, and they are kept apart.

  • Design checks come from our own models with internal verification only: does the rated peak torque fit inside the current limit, does the rotor bridge stay under the lamination's yield at overspeed, and does the open-circuit voltage at top speed stay under the bus voltage (REQ-M1).
  • Validation compares our predictions with ORNL's measurements, once, at the end. The limits were agreed before any data was seen:
QuantityAgreed limitStatus

Efficiency at sample pointswithin 2 percentage pointsFAIL ours 5.2 points higher (median of 393 measured points); 3 of 393 within 2 points

The limits may change only before the ORNL data is opened, and only with a written reason. After that, any change is a documented revision that keeps the earlier verdict. Otherwise the comparison would say nothing.

Lamination bracket

ORNL gives the lamination thickness (0.36 mm) but not the grade, so the final machine was also run on two other 0.35 mm grades. The back-EMF moves by 0.13 % across the three and the torque by under 2 %, far inside the agreed 5 % and 10 % limits: the unknown grade does not limit the blind comparison. Each grade's predictions were written before its run; they are in the case records on the open-circuit and torque pages.

The overspeed stress (424 MPa in the bridge at 7200 rpm) is the same for every grade: it does not depend on the modulus, and the densities differ by 0.7 %. So only the yield changes the verdict. M250-35A, the lower-loss grade, is also the strongest of the three on typical values.

Toolchain

  • Pyleecan (GitHub master 1e940df, in its own Python 3.14 environment) builds the machine and drives FEMM. SciDataTool comes from GitHub master too, because the PyPI release still calls numpy.string_.
  • FEMM 4.2 (x64, 22 Oct 2023 build) solves 2D magnetostatics. Checked first on a 100 A wire, where B = μ0I / (2πr) holds exactly: 0.1-0.5 % on FEMM's default mesh.
  • The harness (this repository) runs each case, checks every point, runs the mesh study, and keeps the inputs, hashes and tool versions with every run.
