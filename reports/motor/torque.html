Torque against current

How much torque does the motor make per amp, and does it reach its rated 400 N m within a copper current-density limit? Each point sweeps the current angle and takes the maximum torque per amp (MTPA).

Assumed design limit: 25 A/mm² rms in the copper, a common short-time limit for an oil-cooled traction motor. With a fill of 0.84 (ORNL's design report) on our slot's 143.0 mm² and 9 turns per slot, that is 333 A rms. No published inverter current rating is used: searching for one risks ORNL's measured torque-current data.

Prior knowledge declared: the 400 N m rating, and ORNL lumped-parameter model values seen in the filter incident (see the overview's blind protocol log).

Method

  • Pyleecan's dq currents are rms. At current I the angle γ runs from the q axis towards negative d: Id = −I sin γ, Iq = I cos γ, from 0 to 70° in 8 steps.
  • At each angle one FEMM simulation over one electrical period (48 steps) gives the torque and the phase flux linkages; the MTPA torque comes from a parabola through the best three angles.
  • 1000 rpm, below base speed, so no voltage limit applies.

MTPA torque against current

M400-50A (inherited)M330-35A (stand-in, the prediction)

The two steels differ by at most 3 %. M330-35A needs less field below 1.7 T, so it gives more torque at low current. Above about 240 A the teeth saturate past 1.8 T, the top of Tata Steel's table. There our M330-35A curve is extrapolated (saturation polarization 2.0 T), and it carries less flux than Pyleecan's M400-50A curve. The prediction above 200 A therefore rests on a stated assumption about deep saturation.
