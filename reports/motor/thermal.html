Study 7 · thermal

Thermal network and continuous rating

What torque can the motor carry continuously at each speed without the winding passing class H or the magnets passing 150 °C, and is the efficiency map's hot corner (windings 100 °C, magnets 80 °C) a state the motor actually reaches? A lumped network built from the geometry, with the losses recomputed at its own temperatures.

Framing (user's decision, 2026-11-22): continuous (steady) envelope from 500 to 6000 rpm; limits 180 °C winding hotspot (class H) and 150 °C magnets; a water-glycol jacket at 65 °C with h = 3000 W/m² K. All three are synthetic: cooling, insulation class and magnet grade are not in the design data we may read. REQ-TH1 (25 kW continuous from 1250 rpm) is our own limit, half the 50 kW peak rating.

Not part of the validation: thermal is outside the agreed ORNL comparison; no ORNL thermal data was opened.

Method

  • Network. Yoke, teeth, slot winding, liner, end windings, end-space air, housing and rotor, each a node at its mean temperature, joined by T-equivalent circuits (Mellor et al. 1991), which are exact for 1D conduction (unit tests against the closed form).
  • Losses. At each speed and torque, the lowest-loss current from the dq tables, with the copper resistance at the network's winding temperatures and the tables blended in magnet temperature between 20 and 80 °C; iterated until the temperatures settle (to under 0.001 K).
  • Inputs from measured machines. The stator-housing interface as a 0.037 mm air gap (Staton et al. 2005, the average of measured machines); end-space convection h = k₁ + k₂ vk₃ (Boglietti and Cavagnino 2007; Micallef's constants); air-gap conduction only (no vortex enhancement, the lower bound); no heat path through the shaft.
  • Check. CalculiX steady conduction on half a slot pitch with the same losses, conductivities and boundary conditions as the network's cross-section, on three meshes.

Result

The magnets, not the winding, set the continuous rating at every speed in the nominal case: with no shaft path and only conduction across the 0.77 mm gap, the rotor floats at the temperature of the tooth tips, the hottest iron. Where the winding mean reaches 100 °C the magnets are at 93-105 °C, so the efficiency map's hot corner (80 °C) is optimistic for them by 13-25 K.

Decision (agreed with the user 2026-11-22 ~17:20): (1) the continuous torque-speed envelope, 500-6000 rpm, that keeps
the winding hotspot within class H (180 C) and the magnets within 150 C under a stated water jacket; (2) whether the
efficiency map's hot corner (windings 100 C, magnets 80 C) is a consistent steady operating state, and where. User's
choices: limits class H 180 C winding hotspot and 150 C magnets (both synthetic: the insulation class and magnet grade
are not in the design data we may read); cooling a water-glycol jacket at 65 C with h 3000 W/m^2 K on the housing,
swept 1000-6000 (synthetic). REQ-TH1's 25 kW is our choice (half the 50 kW peak rating, a common continuous-to-peak
ratio), to turn the envelope into a verdict; the envelope itself is the deliverable. Thermal is not in the agreed
ORNL validation set; no ORNL data was opened for this case (ORNL's thermal report test pages stay sealed).

Sources for the inputs (none from ORNL test data):
- Geometry: models/motor/Toyota_Prius_ORNL.json (Pyleecan SlotW11 outline: shoulder x 81.9727 mm, width 3.1496 mm
  widening to 5.6388 mm at the round end, centre x 111.6653 mm; active slot area 142.96 mm^2, which geometry()
  reproduces to 0.001 mm^2). Copper 76.4 mm^2 per slot (the 2026-11-22 copper correction). End connection 120.6 mm
  per side, extension 38.4 mm (the efficiency case's estimate).
- End-space convection: h = k1 + k2 v^k3, v the rotor peripheral speed (Boglietti and Cavagnino, IEEE TIA 43(5) 2007,
  eq. 1 in the form k1 (1 + k2' v^k3); constants from Altair Flow Simulator's motor end-space table, which cites
  Micallef's thesis (Nottingham) and Boglietti and Cavagnino 2007): winding 15 / 6.0 / 0.9 (= 15 (1 + 0.4 v^0.9)),
  housing 20 / 8.7 / 0.7, rotor 41.4 / 6.22 / 1.0. Measured on TEFC induction motors with finned end rings: our rotor
  has none, so end_space_h_scale 0.5 is a sensitivity scenario.
- Air gap: conduction only (Nu = 2 on 2 g, the laminar Couette regime of Taylor 1935 / Gazley 1958 as summarised by
  Staton, Boglietti and Cavagnino, IEEE TEC 20(3) 2005, sec. VI). The vortex-regime constants could not be read
  (equations are images in the PDF), so no vortex enhancement: the lower bound, conservative for the rotor;
  air_gap_h_factor 3 is a sensitivity scenario.
- Stator-housing interface: an equivalent air gap of 0.037 mm, the average of the measured lamination-housing gaps in
  Staton et al. 2005 Table III (they report 0.01-0.08 mm over machines); swept 0.01 / 0.08.
- Synthetic (typical values, stated): lamination in-plane conductivity 28 W/m K; impregnant 0.25 W/m K, winding
  across the slot by the Maxwell-Garnett / Hashin-Shtrikman lower bound k_imp (1 + phi) / (1 - phi) = 1.02 W/m K
  (phi = 0.606 copper in the winding region), along the slot phi x 390; liner 0.25 mm at 0.2 W/m K; aluminium
  housing wall 6 mm at 160 W/m K, wetted over the stack and both end-winding extensions.
- Stator iron split between teeth and yoke: 0.57 to the teeth, from region areas (283.9 / 328.3 mm^2 per slot pitch)
  weighted by B^2 with B_tooth / B_yoke = 1.25 (tooth flux over the mean tooth width; yoke flux half a sinusoidal
  pole, (2/pi) tau_p / (2 h_y)). Scenarios 0.4 and 0.75.

Hand estimate (before any run of this model; resistances by hand from the geometry above):
  slot winding -> coolant: liner 0.023 K/W, winding across the slot 0.0025, iron about 0.002, interface 0.017
  (the largest after the liner), housing 0.0005, jacket 0.0024: about 0.048 K/W. End windings are tied to the slot
  copper by the copper itself (about 0.008 K/W), so the copper loss mostly leaves through the liner; the end-space
  path (about 0.09 K/W at 3000 rpm, 0.057 at 6000) helps at high speed. Allowable rise about 110 K (hotspot 180, slot
  mean about 170, coolant 65) gives about 2.3 kW of copper loss at about 170 C, about 1.9 kW at the efficiency map's
  100 C resistance: about 225 N m at 1250 rpm from the hot map's losses, less with magnets hotter than 80 C. At 6000
  rpm the iron loss (1.4-2.2 kW at 50-80 N m) loads the interface: about 60-65 N m.

Smoke run (scratch, 500 rpm steps, nominal only; AFTER the predictions above were committed in 1040954). It found two
code defects, fixed before this evidence run: (1) the hot-corner search started at 1 N m, which the operating-point
search cannot hit (0.5 N m band), so it returned nothing; it now starts at 10 N m, the map's lowest torque; (2) the
convergence and magnet-extrapolation figures counted the bisection's probes past thermal runaway (winding 383 C at

from its physical-group *ELSETs, and clockwise triangles are reoriented for CalculiX. The smoke run also showed, and
this case does NOT act on (the model and tolerances are as committed): the magnet limit binds at every speed, not the
