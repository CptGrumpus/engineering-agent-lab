How the drawing was read

Fig. 21's labels are vector text, so page.get_text() (PyMuPDF) gives them exactly. The slot inset gives the depth, the opening, the tooth tip, one slot width and the round end. Because the inset's arrows are placed loosely on a tapered slot, the main view's vector outline was measured too:

  1. Collect the line segments of the stator outline.
  2. Fit a circle to the tooth faces and scale it to the labelled 6.375 in bore.
  3. Intersect the outline with circles of increasing radius. The gaps between crossings are slot and tooth widths.

The only Prius-specific source was Fig. 14 of the 2004 design report, ORNL's measured B-H line of a Prius magnet sample against other magnets. It is a material property, not motor performance, but it is ORNL-measured, so opening it was the user's decision (22:30). Only that figure was rendered; the measured air-gap flux density (Fig. 13) above it on the same page stayed out of the crop.

Why the slot matters

The verified open-circuit run on Pyleecan's machine gives an air-gap field fundamental of 0.893 T. Spread over a tooth at the bore, that is about 1.70 T in Pyleecan's 5.55 mm tooth, past the knee of the steel, and about 1.27 T in the drawing's 7.40 mm tooth. Under load the difference grows, so it matters more for torque than for back-EMF.

Still Pyleecan's

  • Magnet width: 18.9 mm. ORNL/TM-2004/120 gives 19.24 mm with a 5.31 mm thickness, which does not fit the drawn 6.80 mm pocket in this pocket model; together they would move the back-EMF about −0.4 %.
  • Winding: 9 turns per coil, one parallel path, consistent with the 9 conductor bundles per slot in ORNL's text.

Lamination steel

ORNL's design report gives the lamination thickness, 0.014 in (0.36 mm), but not the grade, and Toyota probably used a supplier's own 0.35 mm grade. Until 2026-11-22 the electromagnetics used the M400-50A curve (0.5 mm) that came with Pyleecan's file and was never chosen. Now one stand-in grade serves every property: M330-35A, a standard 0.35 mm EN 10106 grade with full public data, mid-range in its class (Tata Steel typical data, 2009: B-H at 50 Hz to 1.8 T, losses to 2500 Hz, yield 315-335 MPa). The aim is the closest guess to what Toyota used, not the best steel.

  • Above 1.8 T the datasheets stop. The curve continues with the polarization approaching 2.0 T as 1/H, joined with a continuous slope: our assumption, which matters only in the tooth tips at high current.
  • The bracket: M250-35A (lower loss, Tata 2008; less permeable above 1.3 T) and thyssenkrupp powercore 035-190Y390 (high strength, 2025: yield 390 MPa guaranteed, 407 typical). The blind predictions become ranges.
  • Field needed at 1.3 T: 242 A/m for M330-35A against 950 A/m for the inherited M400-50A curve; at 1.5 T, 1027 against 2450. That is why the back-EMF and torque move when the steel changes.
