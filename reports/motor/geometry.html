How the drawing was read

Fig. 21's labels are vector text, so page.get_text() (PyMuPDF) gives them exactly. The slot inset gives the depth, the opening, the tooth tip, one slot width and the round end. Because the inset's arrows are placed loosely on a tapered slot, the main view's vector outline was measured too:

  1. Collect the line segments of the stator outline.
  2. Fit a circle to the tooth faces and scale it to the labelled 6.375 in bore.
  3. Intersect the outline with circles of increasing radius. The gaps between crossings are slot and tooth widths.

From the drawing to Pyleecan's slot parameters

Pyleecan describes this slot as a SlotW11: a tapered slot with a round end, set by seven numbers. The drawing was read to fill exactly those seven; anything the slot type cannot represent was left out.

Why the slot matters

The verified open-circuit run on Pyleecan's machine gives an air-gap field fundamental of 0.893 T. Spread over a tooth at the bore, that is about 1.70 T in Pyleecan's 5.55 mm tooth, past the knee of the M400-50A steel, and about 1.27 T in the drawing's 7.40 mm tooth. Under load the difference grows, so it matters more for torque than for back-EMF.

Still Pyleecan's

  • Magnet pockets (HoleM50). Fig. 21 draws them but gives no dimensions, apart from two red labels, 0.060 in and 0.056 in, that look like the bridges beside the magnets. Pyleecan's bridge is 1.5 mm (0.059 in), close, but what the labels measure is not certain.
  • Magnet material: generic NdFeB, Br 1.24 T at 20 °C, recoil permeability 1.05.
  • Lamination material: M400-50A, a 0.5 mm grade. The real laminations are 0.36 mm (0.014 in), which matters for iron loss and efficiency more than for the field.
  • Winding: 9 turns per coil, one parallel path, consistent with the 9 conductor bundles per slot in ORNL's text.
