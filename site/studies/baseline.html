Study 9 · Python and OpenModelica

Mass-spring-damper baseline

The question

A 1 kg mass on a spring and damper is hit with a sudden 10 N force. How much damping does it need to overshoot by no more than 20 %, settle within 0.5 s and deflect no more than 12 mm? And, more to the point, does the workflow that answers it get a known answer right?

The answer

All 72 checks passTwo tools agree to a few parts per million

A damping ratio of 0.5 or more meets all three requirements. Below that the mass overshoots too far and rings for too long.

This was the first study, and its real job was to prove the workflow on a problem with a known answer. Every result is checked against the exact textbook solution, and a second tool, OpenModelica, solves the same problem independently.

Why it matters

Before trusting a workflow on a problem nobody knows the answer to, it has to get a problem everybody knows the answer to exactly right. The mass-spring-damper is that problem: its response to a step force has a closed-form solution found in any vibration textbook.

Everything the later studies rely on was built and tested here first: a case file that records the question and the tolerances before the run, a solver, a set of automatic checks, a second tool for cross-checking, and a report that ties each requirement to its evidence. If any of that were wrong, this is where it would show.

The model

System
One mass, 1 kg, on a 1000 N/m spring and a viscous damper. It moves in one direction only.
Load
A 10 N force applied suddenly at time zero, from rest.
Damping
Six damping ratios from 0.1 (lightly damped, rings for a long time) to 2.0 (heavily damped, creeps to its final position).
Requirements
Overshoot at most 20 %, settling time at most 0.5 s, peak deflection at most 12 mm.
Tools
A Python model solved with SciPy, and the same system in OpenModelica.

How I checked it

  • Against the exact solution. The simulated motion is compared with the textbook formula at every damping ratio.
  • Energy balance. The work done by the force equals the energy stored in the spring plus the energy the damper has dissipated.
  • The final position. The mass has to come to rest where the spring force equals the applied force: 10 N on a 1000 N/m spring is 10 mm.
  • The solver's own convergence. Tightening the solver's tolerance has to stop changing the answer.
  • A second tool. OpenModelica agrees with the Python model to a few parts per million.

What I caught along the way

  • When I was hooking up OpenModelica, the check that results land on the requested time points failed. OpenModelica was quietly ignoring some of the settings I passed it. I changed how the settings get passed, and that check has been watching for it ever since.

Limits, and what I'd do next

  • It's a textbook problem used to prove the workflow, not a design study.
  • The parameters are made up, and there's nothing to validate against beyond the exact solution.
