Study 17 · Fluid-structure interactionIn progress Flexible Flag Behind a Cylinder

Why it's harderTwo-way fluid-structure interaction, where the flow bends the flag and the bent flag changes the flow.

So farEach half reproduced the published Turek-Hron benchmark on its own before the two were coupled. Coupled and steady, drag and lift are verified at 0.03 % and 0.18 % from the published values. Where the flag swings by itself, the check on the force passed between the two solvers turned out to be defective, so I replaced it and reran both swinging cases on three meshes each. In the faster swing (FSI3), the finest mesh passes every published quantity, the tip's swing within 1.1 %. Across the three meshes, the frequency and the mean drag are verified to within 1 %, and the tip's swing converges onto the published 34.99 mm: extrapolated to a fine mesh, it lands 0.02 % away. The swing of the forces changes too fast between meshes to give a clean estimate, so I report it as it is. A time-step study on the middle mesh, at 1, 0.5 and 0.25 ms, converges the tip's swing, its frequency and its mean position to within 0.75 %. The fluid half's lift had scattered from mesh to mesh, so I rebuilt its meshes as one coarse mesh split evenly twice. In the slower flow (CFD1) that verifies the lift: 0.53 % from the published value, with 0.58 % uncertainty. In the faster one (CFD2) the drag is verified, and the lift ends 1.4 % under the published value, but it closes in too quickly between meshes to count as verified, so a fourth, finer mesh is running.

In the slower, larger swing (FSI2), the finest mesh used to crash 8.8 s into a 15 s run. A rerun that saved every step before the crash showed why: a cell under the flag's tip collapsed, through an oscillation made inside the mesh-motion solve itself. Freezing that solve's stiffness field on the starting mesh cured it, and I reran all three meshes with that one change. All three now reproduce every published quantity within 3.4 %.

OpenFSI2's mesh study fails verification. The frequency converges, to 0.07 %, but the swing of the tip and of the forces creeps away from the benchmark as the mesh gets finer. Five of the six scored quantities miss the 1 % target, and two don't converge in order at all. The benchmark's own values come from finer meshes and another code, so I report this as it is. Two of the three reruns also lost their solid solver just before the end, to a memory fault in the coupling software 14.0 s into the 15, and an unchanged rerun died at the same instant. All three are scored over the same 14 s. In FSI3's time-step study the drag and lift can't be compared, because the finest step's force record wasn't kept, and the coarsest step's solid solver died at 6.9 s, twice and identically, so the three steps are scored to there. I tried three fixes for that crash: a higher iteration limit for the solid, then the coupling's acceleration settings from preCICE's own example of this benchmark in place of the gentler perpendicular-flap example they had been copied from. Each run died a different way within 16 ms of 6.9 s, and all three agree within 0.1 % up to there. What they also share, and what I hadn't looked at, is the solid's undamped time integration. Before every crash, including FSI2's at 14 s, the solid needs more and more iterations per step (2 for most of the run, then up to 14-18), while the run that finished stays flat at 2. That's the same signature that broke the solid-only dynamic test until a small amount of numerical damping cured it, so a run with that one change is testing it. Until it reports, the coarse step is scored on its record to 6.9 s. The runs that finish are unaffected, since every step converged to its tolerance. A crashed solid also used to hang and hold a processor core; it now exits in seconds.

Caught on the wayOne regenerated mesh wrapped a single cell around the flag's tip corner and read the tip deflection 28 % low, with every other check passing. There's now a check on the cells next to the flag, and 24 earlier meshes were scanned with it. Only that one was affected. And the mesh-motion solve that crashed FSI2 gave different answers with different linear solvers: one turned 572 cells inside out, another none, and both reported convergence.
