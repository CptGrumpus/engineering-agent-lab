Study 17 · Fluid-structure interactionIn progress Flexible flag behind a cylinder

Why it's harderTwo-way fluid-structure interaction, where the flow bends the flag and the bent flag changes the flow.

So farEach half reproduced the published Turek-Hron benchmark on its own before the two were coupled. Coupled and steady, drag and lift are verified at 0.03 % and 0.18 % from the published values. In the two cases where the flag swings by itself, every published quantity comes out within 2.1 % and 9 % on one mesh, inside the benchmark's own spread between meshes. Those aren't verified yet: the check on the force passed between the two solvers turned out to be defective, so I've replaced it and the runs will be repeated with a mesh study.

Caught on the wayOne regenerated mesh wrapped a single cell around the flag's tip corner and read the tip deflection 28 % low, with every other check passing. There's now a check on the cells next to the flag, and 24 earlier meshes were scanned with it. Only that one was affected.

Study 19, part 2 · Measured dataIn progress Flow over a backward-facing step

Why it's harderThe flow separates at the step and reattaches downstream, which turbulence models are known to get wrong. It's a classic experiment (Driver and Seegmiller) with measured velocity profiles, skin friction and wall pressure.

The agreed limitsReattachment length within 10 %, velocity profiles within 15 % rms, wall pressure within 0.02 in pressure coefficient. They were set before any comparison data was opened. One thing I did see early, the measured reattachment length, is declared in every case file.

So farThe model, its tests and the outlet-length check are done. The mesh study and the inlet sensitivity are running, then the comparison is made once.

Study 21 · Random vibrationIn progress Bracket arm on a shaker table

The questionDoes the steel bracket arm from study 4 survive a random-vibration qualification: one hour per axis of a flat 20 to 2,000 Hz input, about 8.9 g rms, with 2 % damping? The test level and damping are assumed values, stated as such.

How I'll check itPeak stress at the clamp from the smooth part of the arm, fatigue damage by two independent methods (Steinberg's three-band rule and Dirlik), Miles' equation as a hand check on the first mode, and the solver's own harmonic response against my modal sum.

So farModel, tests and the mesh study's predictions are committed; the mesh study is running.
