Recently Finished

The studies that closed most recently, newest first. As with every study, I picked the independent check before I started modelling and wrote down my predictions before each run. Each one has its own page with the question, the answer, the model, the checks and the limits.

  1. Roller contact, the crown checked in 3DA 3D finite-element model also puts the crowned roller's peak at mid-length, at 635-647 MPa against 650 over six meshes. It agrees with the half-space model on where the peak sits, but its runs are unverified and its margin too thin to confirm the crown independently. Three readings over the limit turned out to be unsettled values, one of them my scoring error.
  2. Flow over a step, in 3D with the tunnel's side wallsThe side walls account for the 2D model's pressure miss: the recovery lands 0.001 from the measurement. The 3D runs stalled short of convergence, so the result is reported as unverified.
  3. Traction motor, as builtDetailed CAD from the client's lamination drawing, with the end turns of all 24 coils. With its mounting ears and notches, the stator core weighs the parts list's 19.05 kg to 0.02 %.
  4. Cold-plate channel, with its side wallsWith a 2 mm aluminium plate the wall peaks at 44.25 °C against 45, so it still passes. A plate under about 0.23 mm would fail at the corners.
  5. O-ring face seal, with the cover bending between its boltsA 4 mm cover lifts about 15 µm at the seal, a fifth of what the squeeze can spare. The flat-cover assumption holds; flatness matters more than thickness.
  6. Pressure vessel nozzle, settled by limit loadThe elastic code check hung on where one line was drawn. A limit-load analysis says the 12 mm nozzle needs no pad: it collapses at 1.675 times the design pressure, against 1.5.
  7. Cable hole in a strap, checked through the thicknessA 3D model puts the stress at mid-thickness 1.5 % above plane stress. 16 mm still passes, by 1 %; the largest hole is about 16.3 mm.
  8. Fan rotor, as a beam model and as a 3D solidA 65 mm shaft clears the critical speeds with 22.9 % to spare as built. The 3D solid shows the beam model gets the hub's effect backwards.
  9. Steel portal frame to AISC 360-22, and its bolted kneePasses, with the code checks held to AISC's own worked examples first. The knee, designed in a second part, needs slip-critical bolts: with bolts that slip, the beam fails.
  10. Flexible flag behind a cylinderFluid and structure solved together, built one verified stage at a time against the Turek-Hron benchmark. Every published value is reproduced; three convergence results are reported as they are.
  11. Gearbox shaft: the shoulder fillet and the keyed gear seatThe drawn step fails in fatigue. With the keyway added, the keyway governs, and the smallest keyed seat that passes is 40 mm.
  12. Cracked steel plate, edge crack and surface flawCrack growth from the smallest flaw inspection finds, with a 3D surface crack checked against Newman-Raju.
  13. Sealed electronics enclosureA capacitor decides the design: paint, a 125 °C part and a 2 W/mK bridge to the lid pass every limit.
  14. O-ring face seal across its tolerance stackPasses at all three corners, with the groove wall adding 40 % to the closing force.
  15. Traction motor against its test dataModelled from the drawings with nothing tuned; the 18 % gap to the lab test traced to the magnets.
