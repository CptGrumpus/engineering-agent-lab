Example 11 · Heat transfer in OpenFOAM

Cold-plate channel heat transfer

The question. A cold-plate passage, 1 mm deep, carries water at 25 °C and 0.1 m/s. Electronics heat both walls at 30 kW/m² over 50 mm. Does the wall stay at or under 45 °C, does the coolant warm by no more than 10 K, and how much heat flux could the passage take?

Coolant rise 7.2 K: verifiedWall 44.0–44.1 °C: mesh uncertainty openRated about 31 kW/m²

The coolant warms by 7.2 K, inside its 10 K budget, and that is verified: the energy balance closes to a part in a trillion on every mesh. The wall peaks at 44.0 to 44.1 °C on all three meshes, 0.86 to 0.97 K under the limit, and matches the independent reference within 0.6 %. But the peak isn't yet settling smoothly with refinement, so the mesh-convergence check can't size its uncertainty and that verdict stays formally withheld. At 40 cells the wall reaches 45 °C at about 31 kW/m².

How I know it's right

  • Against an independent solution. The thermal-entry (Graetz) problem, solved separately and checked against the textbook fully developed Nusselt number and the energy balance. The CFD wall temperature matches it within 0.03 to 0.6 % of the rise.
  • Energy balance and convergence. Heat in equals heat carried out to a part in a trillion, and the temperature field stops changing to 10⁻¹¹ of the rise.
  • Why the reference matters. The flow is still thermally developing at the end of the heating (Nusselt 8.34, not the textbook 8.24), so a hand calculation assuming developed flow would be wrong.

What the checks caught

  • An overshoot exactly where the answer is. The standard scheme overshoots by 0.06 to 0.11 K at the last heated cell, where the peak sits. It errs on the safe side, but it is why the peak doesn't converge smoothly with the mesh.
  • Fixes that only hid the problem. Four bounded schemes that remove the overshoot all dithered from one iteration to the next and never closed the energy balance; under-relaxation only masked it. I went back to the scheme that converges exactly, let the checks bound its bias, kept every failed run, and never changed a tolerance.
