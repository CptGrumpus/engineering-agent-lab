The agent designs a lighter bracket

The steel bracket from study 1 (200 mm long, 1 kN at the tip) now sits in a vibrating assembly. Its bending stress has to stay under 100 MPa for fatigue, the tip can deflect at most 1.0 mm, and the 20 mm width is fixed by the mounting. An optimiser run by the agent picks the root and tip heights of a linear taper to make it as light as possible, and every design it tries is built, meshed, solved and checked like any other study.

How the loop works

  1. ProposeCOBYLA, an optimiser that needs no gradients and handles limits, picks a root and a tip height.
  2. BuildCadQuery draws the tapered solid and exports a STEP file. Gmsh meshes it with quadratic tetrahedra.
  3. SolveCalculiX computes the deflection and stresses under the 1 kN tip load.
  4. CheckThe study's full set of checks runs. A design that fails any of them counts as infeasible, whatever its numbers say.
  5. RepeatThe optimiser steps toward lighter designs that pass until its step shrinks below 0.01 mm. The whole search is one command.
  • Rejected designs can't steer the search
  • 0.5 % margin kept for mesh error
  • Predictions written before the run
What a root-only check misses

The stress peak moves into the span

Once the taper is steeper than 2 to 1, the bending stress peaks inside the span, not at the root. An optimiser that only checked the root would have kept thinning the tip and handed back a bracket that fails in the middle. The peak at the clamped corner can't be used either: it grows without limit as the mesh gets finer, the trap from study 1.

A design outside the model

Candidate 8 was thrown out

One long step reached a 390 g design that deflected 2.7 mm, 1.35 % of the span. That's past the small-deflection limit of a linear analysis, so the checks rejected it, and the optimiser treated it as infeasible and stepped back. The run is kept as evidence.

An answer sitting on its limit

Room left for mesh error

The optimiser drives the stress right onto the limit at whatever mesh size it's using, so a finer mesh could tip the answer over. I set a 0.5 % margin before running. The mesh study at the optimum then measured the real error at 0.005 %, and the stress stays 0.49 % under 100 MPa with it included.

How I checked it, and what it doesn't cover
  • Every candidate goes through the full set of checks. The CAD volume is compared with the closed form, the applied load and tip area are read back from the mesh, and the reactions have to balance. The tip deflection has to match tapered Timoshenko beam theory, which it does within 0.6 %, and the FE surface stress has to match M/Z along the span, within 0.7 % as a fit and 1.5 % point by point.
  • A separate mesh study at the optimum puts the mesh uncertainty at 0.005 % on the stress and 0.016 % on the deflection, and the requirement is still met with that added.
  • I also optimised the bracket independently with beam theory, using the same limits and margin. It lands at 545.2 g against the FE optimum's 546.0 g, a 0.13 % difference, with the heights within 0.7 %.
  • I wrote down predictions before running it (the heights, the mass, which limit would govern and where the stress would peak), and they all held.

It's a linear taper only, so a curved profile would be lighter still. The joint at the root needs its own assessment, and there's no lateral-torsional buckling check yet.
