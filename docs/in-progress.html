Study 17 · Fluid-structure interactionIn progress Flexible flag behind a cylinder

Why it's harderTwo-way fluid-structure interaction, where the flow bends the flag and the bent flag changes the flow.

So farEach half has to reproduce the published Turek-Hron benchmark before the two are coupled. The solid's sag matches to 0.01 %, and it swings at 1.095 Hz against the published 1.096 to 1.100. The fluid's drag matches to 0.07 % and 0.014 % at the two flow speeds. Lift is within 0.7 % but scatters from mesh to mesh, so I'm holding that verdict back. The first coupled case has run: its drag converges to within 0.03 % of the benchmark, and the flag tip lands within 1 %, but the tip still drifts as the mesh is refined and the lift carries noise from the coupling, so it isn't verified yet. A revision is running.

Study 20 · Electromagnetics · test dataIn progress A production traction motor, checked blind against test data

Why it's harderThree kinds of physics on one machine: torque, back-EMF and efficiency from the electromagnetics, a thermal network, and rotor stress at overspeed. It's modelled on the 2004 Toyota Prius motor.

How I'll check itInternal checks first: an analytic air-gap field, the power balance at every operating point, and mesh convergence. Then the portfolio's first comparison with measured data, Oak Ridge National Laboratory's tests, opened only after my predictions are written down.

So farEvery blind prediction is now written down: back-EMF 110.9 V per 1,000 rpm, the torque curve from 119 to 606 N m (50 to 350 A), and an efficiency map peaking at 97.4 %. The real steel grade isn't published, so I ran three candidates; they move the back-EMF by 0.13 % and the torque by under 2 %, so the unknown grade won't decide the comparison.

Design checksThe rated 400 N m fits inside the copper's current limit, and the rated 50 kW holds all the way to 6,000 rpm (58.6 kW). Two results are failures a designer needs to know about. If the inverter stops switching at top speed, the magnets alone would drive about 1,040 V against the 500 V supply. And at 20 % overspeed the thin steel bridges that hold the magnets reach 424 MPa against the lamination's 335 MPa yield. Next, the test data is opened once and every prediction is scored against it.
